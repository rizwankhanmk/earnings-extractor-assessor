FRP Holdings, Inc. Reports Fiscal 2026 Third Quarter Results
Mining Royalty Revenue Rises 10%; Multifamily NOI Remains Pressured; Industrial Leasing Remains the Near-Term Priority
JACKSONVILLE, FL., November 5, 2026 – FRP Holdings, Inc. (NASDAQ: FRPH), a full-service real estate investment and development company with four distinct business segments including Multifamily, Industrial and Commercial, Development, and Mining Royalty Lands, today reported financial results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026. Key results for the quarter ended September 30, 2026 include:
Q3 2026 Financial Highlights & Recent Developments:
•Multifamily portfolio average occupancy was 93.0% across 1,827 units; pro rata NOI declined 12% year-over-year as concessions and operating expenses increased, and performance weakened at our Washington, DC properties.
•Average Industrial & Commercial occupancy, excluding Chelsea, was 71.4% versus 72.4% a year earlier; quarter-end occupancy was 74.3%
•Mining royalties were up 10% as a result of increases in both volume (up 6.9%) and revenue per ton (up 3.0%)
•Industrial deliveries are advancing, with leasing and stabilization required to convert development investment into recurring NOI and FFO
•Executed 126,682 square feet of industrial leasing activity, the most active quarter since Q2 2021 and more total leasing activity than Q2 2025-Q2 2026 combined.
“Third quarter results reflect continued leasing challenges in our Washington, DC multifamily assets and Maryland industrial portfolio, higher G&A following the Altman acquisition, and lower interest income as we deploy cash into development and balances in our lending ventures decline,” said John Baker III, CEO of FRP Holdings. “Mining remained a strong contributor, with royalty revenue increasing approximately 10% year-over-year. Our priority is building industrial scale and converting our investments into recurring NOI, supported by the contribution of our multifamily and mining businesses. We executed approximately 127,000 square feet of industrial leasing activity this quarter, our most active quarter since Q2 2021. Our immediate focus is carrying that activity forward and improving performance across our operating portfolio.”

Baker continued, “Chelsea, Lakeland, Davie, and Camp Lake together represent approximately 1.0 million square-feet of industrial space. We expect Lakeland and Davie to be substantially complete in the fourth quarter of 2026, followed by Camp Lake in the first quarter of 2027. As these properties move through shell completion and lease-up, interest expense and property operating costs previously capitalized during construction, along with depreciation will weigh on reported earnings until rental income increases. At stabilization, we estimate these four assets will generate approximately $12 million in annual recurring pro rata NOI and $7 million in annual FFO. These estimates represent annual contributions at stabilization, and their timing will depend on leasing and ultimately rent commencement. We are also focused on leasing vacancies at Cranberry Business Park, which we estimate could contribute approximately $1 million in additional annual recurring NOI and FFO at full occupancy. Our three merchant build projects located in Delray, Florida and Hamilton and Parsippany, New Jersey, totaling 510,307 square feet where FRP owns a minority interest, are in the early stages of lease-up. We intend to monetize these investments following stabilization and recycle the proceeds into future industrial opportunities.”

Q3 Consolidated Results of Operations
•Net loss attributable to the Company of $(165,000), or $(0.01) per share, versus net income of $662,000, or $0.03 per share.
•Pro rata NOI was down 5% to $9.0 million versus $9.5 million in Q3 2025, with the decline driven by lower Multifamily, Industrial and Commercial, and Development NOI, partially offset by higher Mining Royalty NOI
•Total revenues were $11.0 million, up 1.7%, as a 10% increase in mining royalty revenue and $49,000 of joint venture management fee revenue from the Altman platform helped to offset a 3% decline in lease revenue
•G&A increased $1,007,000 versus Q3 2025, driven by higher personnel costs, higher legal fees and integration expenses following the Altman acquisition, partially offset by $365,000 of increased labor capitalization
•Investment income decreased $1,018,000, reflecting $761,000 of lower interest income on cash balances due to lower average balances and interest rates, and $257,000 of lower lending venture income as outstanding loan balances declined.
Multifamily Segment
•Pro rata NOI in this segment was $4.0 million, down $542,000 or 12% versus Q3 2025. Although overall portfolio average occupancy was 93%, higher concessions and operating expenses, together with weaker performance at our Washington, DC properties, reduced NOI.


•Bryant Street’s NOI was down $273,000, accounting for over 50% of the decline in NOI year-over-year, driven by a 240 bps decline in occupancy to 91.0%, along with higher operating expenses and increased concessions; Dock 79’s NOI was down $121,000 despite occupancy improving 120 bps to 95.0% due to higher rent concessions and increased operating expenses; The Maren’s NOI was down $71,000 with occupancy declining 50 bps to 93.6%; The Verge’s NOI was down $49,000 with occupancy declining 110 bps to 90.9%
•Our Greenville assets remained steady with average overall occupancy above 95%
•Renewal rate increases in our DC assets averaged 2.5%; while those in our Greenville assets averaged 4.5%
Industrial and Commercial Segment
•This segment’s NOI was $651,000, down $253,000 or 28% versus Q3 2025, primarily reflecting the expiration of the former Vulcan land lease at 21st Street ($183,000), along with vacancies in our Maryland assets
•Excluding Chelsea, average occupancy for the quarter in our 9 existing in-service buildings was 71.4% versus 72.4% in Q3 2025, with the decline driven by non-renewing lease expirations. At quarter-end, the occupancy in our 9 existing in-service buildings was 74.3% as we added a new tenant during the quarter.
•During Q3 2026, we signed 61,065 square feet of new leases, 59,828 square feet of renewals, and a 5,789-square-foot three-month extension, totaling 126,682 square feet. Leasing approximately 399,000 square feet of available space in our Maryland portfolio, which includes 13,710 square feet of office, remains a primary near-term opportunity to increase NOI
Mining Royalty Segment
•NOI was $4.1 million, up $359,000 or 10% year-over-year, with both volume and pricing trending favorably
Development and Active Pipeline
•At our Harford County residential lending venture, we sold 23 lots versus 20 lot sales in Q3 2025 (and have now sold 271 of the 344 lots and booked $7.8 million of interest and profit to date)
•We expect our Lakeland and Davie warehouses to be substantially complete by the first of the year with Camp Lake to follow in the first quarter of 2027
•The Woven project in Greenville, SC (214 units with 13,500 sq ft of ground floor retail) is under construction with substantial completion expected late 2027
•Estero Phase 1 in the Naples/Ft. Myers, FL market is also under construction (296 multifamily units and 28,745 sq ft of retail) with substantial completion also expected late 2027
Merchant Platform
•This was the third full quarter following the October 21, 2025, closing of the Altman Logistics Property acquisition
•The acquired projects include warehouses in Delray Beach, FL (199,476 sq ft, completed Q1 2026; additional land for 392,976 sq ft in two warehouses); Hamilton, NJ (170,800 sq ft, completed Q2 2026); Parsippany, NJ (140,031 sq ft, completed Q3 2026); and Southwest Ranches, FL (335,617 sq ft; land acquired July 2026)
•The acquisition added origination, construction, and asset management capabilities to support the growth of FRP’s industrial platform

Nine-Month Consolidated Results of Operations
•The Company reported a net loss of $1,111,000 or $(0.06) per share, versus net income of $2,950,000 or $0.16 per share in the first nine months of 2025
•Pro rata NOI was down 5% to $27.3 million versus $28.6 million in the first nine months of 2025, with the decline driven by lower Multifamily, Industrial and Commercial and Development segment NOI, partially offset by higher Mining Royalty segment NOI
•Total revenues were $32.6 million, up 2.2%, as a 12% increase in mining royalty revenue and $407,000 of joint venture management fee revenue from the Altman platform helped to offset a 5% decline in lease revenue
•G&A increased $3,317,000 versus the first nine months of 2025, driven by higher personnel costs, higher audit fees, higher legal fees and integration expenses following the Altman acquisition, partially offset by $966,000 of increased labor capitalization
•Investment income decreased $3,002,000, reflecting $2,030,000 of lower interest income on cash balances due to lower average balances and interest rates, and $972,000 of lower lending venture income as outstanding loan balances declined

Multifamily Segment — Nine Months
•Pro rata NOI in this segment was $12.4 million, down $1,509,000 or 11% versus the first nine months of 2025, as portfolio-wide average occupancy declined to 92.8% from 93.7%
•The decline was predominantly concentrated in our DC assets: Bryant Street’s NOI was down $596,000 to $4,134,000 with occupancy declining 160 bps to 91.9%; The Verge’s NOI was down $309,000 to $1,793,000 with occupancy declining 260 bps to 90.3%; Dock 79’s NOI was down $364,000 to $2,474,000 with occupancy declining 250 bps to 92.5%; and The Maren’s NOI was down $221,000 to $2,320,000 with occupancy declining 60 bps to 93.2%


•Our Greenville assets remained steady with average occupancy above 95%
•Renewal rate increases in our DC assets averaged 2.6%; while those in our Greenville assets averaged 3.0%
Industrial and Commercial Segment — Nine Months
•This segment’s NOI was $2,025,000, down $1,028,000 or 34% versus the first nine months of 2025, reflecting the March 31, 2026 expiration of the former Vulcan land lease at 21st Street ($365,000) and vacancies in our Maryland assets
•Total revenues were $3,220,000, down $730,000 or 18%
•Our operating profit before G&A was $246,000, down $1,181,000 or 83%, including $298,000 of Chelsea warehouse depreciation and carrying costs
•Leasing up the Maryland portfolio remains the primary near-term NOI driver for the Company
Mining Royalty Segment — Nine Months
•NOI was $12.0 million, up $1,310,000 or 12% year-over-year, driven both by royalty tons (up 2.6%) and higher revenue per ton (up 10.2%).
Development Segment — Nine Months
•Operating profit before G&A was $514,000, up $2,316,000 versus an operating loss of $1,802,000 in the first nine months of 2025
•The improvement was driven by $407,000 of joint venture management fee revenue from the Altman platform and the prior year including $1,993,000 of Altman acquisition expenses, partially offset by $195,000 less capitalized real estate taxes
