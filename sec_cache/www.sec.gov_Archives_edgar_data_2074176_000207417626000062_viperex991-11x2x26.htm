VIPER ENERGY, INC., A SUBSIDIARY OF DIAMONDBACK ENERGY, INC., REPORTS THIRD QUARTER 2026 FINANCIAL AND OPERATING RESULTS

MIDLAND, Texas, November 2, 2026 (GLOBE NEWSWIRE) -- Viper Energy, Inc. (NASDAQ:VNOM) (“Viper,” “we,” “our” or the “Company”), a subsidiary of Diamondback Energy, Inc. (NASDAQ:FANG) (“Diamondback”), today announced financial and operating results for the third quarter ended September 30, 2026.


THIRD QUARTER FINANCIAL HIGHLIGHTS
•Net income of $300 million; Net income margin of 45%
•Adjusted EBITDA1 of $596 million; Adjusted EBITDA margin1 of 90%
•Cash flows from operating activities of $447 million; Operating cash flow margin of 68%
•Free Cash Flow1 of $520 million; Free Cash Flow margin1 of 79%
•Declared Q3 2026 base cash dividend of $0.50 per Class A common share
•Repurchased approximately 2.1 million Class A shares for approximately $90 million
•Total debt of $2.4 billion as of September 30, 2026; Net debt1 of $2.4 billion as of September 30, 2026

THIRD QUARTER OPERATIONAL HIGHLIGHTS
•Average daily production of 69,076 bo/d (141,152 boe/d)
•As previously announced, on July 1, 2026, completed the Riverbend Acquisition
•On September 1, 2026, closed the previously announced 2026 Drop Down of approximately 1,000 net

MCELROY RANCH ACQUISITION
During the month of September, in two separate but related transactions, Viper acquired mineral and royalty interests from third-party sellers for an aggregate purchase price of approximately $529 million, subject to customary post-closing adjustments and including certain transaction costs and $49 million of deferred consideration payable in equal quarterly installments through June 2029 (collectively, the “McElroy Ranch Acquisition”). Acquisition highlights are as follows:

•Approximately 7,359 net royalty acres located in Upton and Crane Counties
•Concentrated ownership of an approximately 12% net royalty interest across roughly 60,000 gross acres in an expected core geologic fairway of Barnett development in the western Midland Basin
•Asset is 96% undeveloped with an expected 40.9 net inventory locations
◦Only 21 currently producing gross (1.7 net) horizontal locations across the entire Ranch
◦Chevron operates approximately 27% of the total net inventory, including approximately 50% of net undeveloped Barnett inventory
◦Diamondback operates approximately 15% of the total net inventory
◦Concentrated nature of mineral ownership will enable potential trades to accelerate development in the coming years
•Offsetting Barnett wells that have at least 12 months of production data indicate an average estimated ultimate recovery of approximately 80 bo/ft
•Stable production of approximately 400 bo/d from existing vertical wells
•Forecasted peak production on the Ranch in the range of 5,000 bo/d over the next decade

PENDING BUCKHORN ACQUISITION
On September 14, 2026, Viper entered into a definitive purchase and sale agreement to acquire certain mineral and royalty interests from a group of entities collectively called Buckhorn Minerals (the “Pending Buckhorn Acquisition”) in exchange for $280 million cash and approximately 1.8 million shares of Viper’s Class A common stock, subject to customary closing adjustments. The cash portion of the transaction is expected to be funded through a combination of cash on hand and borrowings under the Company’s revolving credit facility. The transaction is expected to close in the fourth quarter of 2026, subject to the satisfaction of customary closing conditions. Acquisition highlights are as follows:

•1,552 net royalty acres, substantially all of which are in the Midland Basin; approximately 80% overlap with existing Viper acreage position
•Midland Basin primary operators include ExxonMobil (47%) and Diamondback (29%)
•Asset is 61% undeveloped with an expected 17.4 net inventory locations
◦Approximately 20% of Midland Basin acreage in units with zero producing horizontal wells; expected to support efficient full stack development in coming years
◦47% of net inventory in Viper’s estimation of remaining top quartile inventory in the Midland Basin, and 70% of net inventory within the top half of remaining inventory (as determined by expected internal rate of return for the respective operators)
•Expected next 12 months’ average daily oil production of approximately 1,500 bo/d
•Expect pro forma net debt at year end 2026 of approximately $2.4 billion, equating to pro forma leverage of approximately 1.0x last twelve months Adjusted EBITDA3

OTHER RECENT EVENTS AND FORWARD OUTLOOK
•Inclusive of the Pending Buckhorn Acquisition, initiating average daily production guidance for Q4 2026 of 70,000 to 71,000 bo/d (141,500 to 143,500 boe/d)
•Increasing average daily production guidance for full year 2026 to 67,250 to 67,750 bo/d (137,000 to 138,000 boe/d)
•Repurchased approximately 0.4 million Class A shares in Q4 2026 (to date) for approximately $17 million

“Viper’s momentum carried through the third quarter, with average daily oil production growing more than six percent quarter over quarter on the strength of recent acquisitions and continued development activity from both Diamondback and our third-party operators. Reflecting this continued momentum, we are again increasing our full year 2026 production guidance to 67,500 barrels of oil per day at the midpoint. The quarter also marked the first under our new return of capital framework, through which we declared our increased base dividend of $0.50 per Class A share while continuing to opportunistically repurchase shares,” said Kaes Van’t Hof, Chief Executive Officer of Viper.

Mr. Van’t Hof continued, “The McElroy Ranch Acquisition and Pending Buckhorn Acquisition announced today represent the continued execution of our differentiated consolidation strategy. The acquisitions combine to extend the quality and duration of our inventory, adding nearly 60 net locations across two largely undeveloped assets. The McElroy Ranch asset brings concentrated mineral ownership in an expected core fairway of emerging Barnett development, while the Pending Buckhorn Acquisition adds high-quality Midland Basin inventory with approximately 80% overlap with our existing footprint. Notably, we funded, or expect to fund, these acquisitions primarily with cash, delivering on the commitment we made when we revised our return of capital framework to pursue accretive M&A with minimal reliance on equity. These acquisitions add years of visible development to Viper’s portfolio and reinforce the durability of our long-term per-share growth outlook.”

FINANCIAL UPDATE

Viper’s third quarter 2026 average unhedged realized prices were $87.65 per barrel of oil, $0.96 per Mcf of natural gas and $24.02 per barrel of natural gas liquids, resulting in a total equivalent realized price of $50.52/boe.

Viper’s third quarter 2026 average hedged realized prices were $86.55 per barrel of oil, $0.66 per Mcf of natural gas and $24.02 per barrel of natural gas liquids, resulting in a total equivalent realized price of $49.51/boe.

During the third quarter of 2026, the Company recorded total operating income of $661 million and consolidated net income (including non-controlling interest) of $300 million.

As of September 30, 2026, the Company had a cash balance of $10 million and total debt outstanding (excluding debt issuance costs, discounts and premiums) of $2.4 billion, resulting in net debt (as defined and reconciled below) of $2.4 billion. Viper’s outstanding long-term debt as of September 30, 2026 consisted of $500 million in aggregate principal amount of its 4.900% Senior Notes due 2030, $1.1 billion in aggregate principal amount of its 5.700% Senior Notes due 2035 and $810 million of borrowings on its revolving credit facility, leaving approximately $1.2 billion available for future borrowings and approximately $1.2 billion of total liquidity.




THIRD QUARTER 2026 CASH DIVIDEND & CAPITAL RETURN PROGRAM

Viper announced today that the Company’s Board of Directors (the “Board”) declared a base cash dividend of $0.50 per Class A common share for the third quarter of 2026, payable on November 19, 2026 to Class A common stockholders of record at the close of business on November 12, 2026.

During the third quarter of 2026, Viper repurchased approximately 2.1 million shares of the Company’s Class A common stock for an aggregate purchase price of approximately $90 million, excluding excise tax (average price of $43.01 per share).

In total, since the initiation of Viper’s common stock repurchase program on November 9, 2020 through October 30, 2026, the Company has repurchased approximately 26.1 million shares of common stock (including both Class A shares and Class B shares paired with OpCo Units) for an aggregate purchase price of approximately $844 million, excluding excise tax (average price of $32.30 per share) and has approximately $906 million remaining on its share buyback authorization. Future cash dividends and stock repurchases are at the discretion of the Board and are subject to a number of factors discussed in Viper’s reports filed with the U.S. Securities and Exchange Commission (“SEC”).

The 1,965 gross wells currently in the process of active development are those wells that have been spud and are expected to be turned to production within approximately the next six to eight months. NON-GAAP FINANCIAL MEASURES

ADJUSTED EBITDA AND ADJUSTED EBITDA MARGIN

Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA margin are supplemental non-GAAP (as defined below) financial measures that are used by management and external users of our financial statements, such as industry analysts, investors, lenders and rating agencies. The Company defines Adjusted EBITDA as net income (loss) attributable to the Company, plus net income (loss) attributable to non-controlling interest (“net income (loss)”) before interest expense, net, non-cash share-based compensation expense, depreciation, depletion and amortization, impairment, non-cash (gain) loss on derivative instruments, provision for (benefit from) income taxes and other non-cash or non-recurring operating expenses. The Company defines Adjusted EBITDA margin as Adjusted EBITDA divided by total operating income. Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA margin are not measures of net income as determined by United States’ generally accepted accounting principles (“GAAP”). Management believes Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA margin are useful because they allow them to evaluate the Company’s operating performance and compare the results of its operations from period to period without regard to its financing methods or capital structure. Adjusted EBITDA should not be considered as an alternative to, or more meaningful than, net income, royalty income, cash flow from operating activities or any other measure of financial performance or liquidity presented as determined in accordance with GAAP. Adjusted EBITDA margin should not be considered as an alternative to, or more meaningful than, net income margin, gross margin, or any other measure of financial performance or liquidity presented as determined in accordance with GAAP. Certain items excluded from Adjusted EBITDA and Adjusted EBITDA margin are significant components in understanding and assessing a company’s financial performance, such as a company’s cost of capital and tax structure, as well as the historic costs of depreciable assets, none of which are components of Adjusted EBITDA or Adjusted EBITDA margin.

OPERATING CASH FLOW BEFORE WORKING CAPITAL CHANGES, FREE CASH FLOW AND FREE CASH FLOW MARGIN

Operating cash flow before working capital changes, which is a non-GAAP financial measure, represents net cash provided by operating activities as determined under GAAP without regard to changes in operating assets and liabilities. The Company believes operating cash flow before working capital changes is a useful measure of the Company’s ability to generate cash to fund acquisition activities, service debt or pay dividends. The Company also uses this measure because changes in operating assets and liabilities relate to the timing of cash receipts and disbursements that the Company may not control and may not relate to the period in which the operating activities occurred.

The Company defines Free Cash Flow, which is a non-GAAP financial measure, as operating cash flow before working capital changes in excess of capital expenditures. The Company defines Free Cash Flow margin as Free Cash Flow divided by total operating income. The Company believes that Free Cash Flow and Free Cash Flow margin are useful to investors as they provide a measure to compare cash flow from operating activities across periods on a consistent basis. These measures should not be considered as alternatives to, or more meaningful than, net cash provided by operating activities as an indicator of liquidity. The Company's computations of Free Cash Flow and Free Cash Flow margin may not be comparable to other similarly titled measures of other companies.

Management uses net debt to determine the Company’s outstanding debt obligations that would not be readily satisfied by its cash and cash equivalents on hand.
